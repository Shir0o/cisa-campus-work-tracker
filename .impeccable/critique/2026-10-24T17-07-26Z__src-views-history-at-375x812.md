# Critique: Looking back — the team's record at 375×812

Target: `/history`, at 375×812, against the emulator through the dev server (`VITE_E2E_MODE=true`, `qa-db`). 31 moments across 18 day markers, written as the app writes them (ISO strings from `logActivity`, plus the Firestore `Timestamp` shapes the webhook, the batch ingest and `tagEngine` write).

**The brief says “sign in as Trainee”; a Trainee cannot open this route.** `canAccessRoute('manager', '/history')` is false: Trainees are an allowlist in `src/lib/permissions.ts` (`['/', '/directory', '/board', '/messages', '/questions', '/feedback', '/contact', '/bible-study/read', '/bible-study/present']`), and `/history` is not on it — even though `ROUTE_MIN_ROLE['/history']` says `manager`. Verified live: signed in as `trainee.e2e@example.com`, opening `/history` redirects to `/` (`Looking back` never renders). So this pass measured the page as the role that actually owns it — the Full-timer — and filed the role question as **#1825** rather than changing a permission the role matrix owns. The page's copy (“the team”, “the people in our care”) is written for the Full-timer whose record it is; a Trainee's own history is the Home card.

## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** This is not a generic activity feed. It is built as a *record of care*: one unbroken vertical thread, a tonal node per kind of work (steps, prayer, talk, gathering), the person's name in ink and the verb in the page's own pastoral voice, the witness quote set as an italic margin note. “We write it down so nothing — and no one — is forgotten” is the page's thesis and the desktop card says it out loud. The mobile branch keeps all of it.

Where it drifts is the usual phone-pass drift, but with a sharper edge than the other screens: **the phone does its own date formatting.** The desktop used the shared `bucketFor`/`bucketLabel` vocabulary; the phone imported `date-fns`'s `format()` with a literal `'EEEE, MMMM d'` and `'h:mm a'`, which is English by construction, and built its own per-day labels in `dayInfo` — the one place where “this week” became a weekday label and “older” became a date label, so the column changed meaning mid-scroll. And because `activities.createdAt` is typed `string` but is written as a Firestore `Timestamp` by three server paths, the phone's `format(new Date(createdAt))` was the only reader in the app that could not survive the app's own data.

**Deterministic scan.** `impeccable detect` is not installed in this worktree, so no CLI scan was run; every finding below is from the live DOM and the accessibility tree.

**Visual overlays.** Live renders at 375×812 (device scale 2) in English and Spanish, the filter sheet, a filtered-empty list, an empty record, a dark-mode render, and a screen crashed by one `Timestamp`-shaped row. All states were produced by writing to the emulator, not by mocking the component.

## Overall Impression

The page has the best-written sentences in the app and the weakest data plumbing under them. Read cold, it is a warm, quiet record; read by a reader whose team logs through the webhook, it is a white screen. Read by a screen reader, it is a page with one button and thirty-one paragraphs. Read in Spanish, it is Spanish chrome wrapped around an English diary.

The fixes are all small and local: one date helper that every reader shares, one year, one search field the desktop already had, one real `<button>` per person, one dialog role, two honest empty states, and one branch in the vocabulary. Nothing here needs a redesign.

## What's Working

1. **The spine, on a phone.** A 1px rule, a 20px tonal node per moment, and the day marker cut into the line. It gives 31 moments one reading direction without a table, a second column, or a card per row.
2. **The voice.** “Priya gave thanks for an answered prayer for Held Burden.” “Zion started praying for Lila Mora.” “Caleb went round to Lila Mora — at their flat on Osbourne Road.” This is a record written by a team, not a `logs` table rendered.
3. **The witness quote.** Setting the logged description as an indented italic margin note separates *what happened* from *what was said*, and it survives at 13.5px/7.03:1.
4. **One filter control.** A single 335×44 “Filter history” button with a count badge, and applied filters as removable chips — far better than four always-visible controls on a phone.
5. **Person detail is a page, not a popup.** `onOpenContact` swaps the view and back returns here (`usePreserveScroll`), so the stream is not lost.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] One server-written row took the whole screen down
- **What**: `activities.createdAt` is written as an ISO string by `logActivity` (`src/lib/firebase.ts:169`) but as a Firestore `Timestamp` by `server.ts` (`:840`, `:925`, `:1028`, `:1096`, `:1702`, `:1732`, `:2333`) and by `tagEngine` (`:292`, `:309`, `:500`, `:516`). `HistoryMobile` rendered `format(new Date(a.createdAt), 'h:mm a')`; `new Date(Timestamp)` is Invalid Date and `date-fns` throws `RangeError: Invalid time value`, which the error boundary turns into a full-screen **“Something went wrong — Invalid time value”**. Reproduced live by writing one such row to the emulator: the header, the filter and all 31 moments disappeared, and Reload returned to the same screen. Removing the row restored the page.
- **Why it matters**: this is the app's memory surface and the *only* consumer of `activities` that crashed on the app's own data. A team that logs through the SMS/GroupMe webhook — the path that writes `serverTimestamp()` — loses Looking back entirely, and the error reads like a broken app, not a broken record.
- **Fix**: one reader, `historyMs()` in `src/views/historyTime.ts`, built on the shared `parseMs` that the rest of the app already uses. `dayMark()` labels with it, the phone prints `clockLabel()`, and `historyRows()` orders and groups with it. A row the app cannot date still renders — it just carries no marker — instead of taking the screen with it. Tests: a `Timestamp`-shaped value yields a clock and a marker, and the view renders a `Timestamp` row instead of throwing (that test reproduced `RangeError: Invalid time value` before the fix).
- **Also found while testing it**: Firestore sorts by *type* before value, so a collection holding both ISO strings and Timestamps comes back in two blocks — the same day can arrive twice and the stream can run backwards. With one such row present the console logged `Encountered two children with the same key, 'd:Sat Oct 24 2026'`. `historyRows()` now orders by the parsed instant, so each day is announced once and the stream is monotonic regardless of how the two shapes interleave. Test: three rows (today-ISO, yesterday-ISO, today-Timestamp) produce two unique markers and newest-first items.

### [P1] Two different years printed the same day marker, and the label changed sides at seven days
- **What**: no day marker carried a year, and `dayInfo` changed which value went in the label column at the 7-day boundary. Measured over 31 moments: `Today October 24`, `Yesterday October 23`, `Thursday October 22` … `Sunday October 18`, then **`October 16 Friday`**, `October 15 Thursday` … With three older rows added, the record produced **two markers reading “September 19”** — one Saturday, one Friday, a year apart — and a third reading “August 19 Tuesday”.
- **Why it matters**: a record that runs for years is exactly where a missing year is unreadable. Two identical markers look like duplicated content or a scroll bug, and the swap of label and sub-label means the eye cannot scan one column of dates down a long list.
- **Fix**: the marker is `Today`/`Yesterday` + date, or date + weekday, always in that order, and carries the year whenever it is not this year (`September 19, 2025` / `19 de septiembre de 2025`). Today and Yesterday are translated rather than borrowed from the English `bucketLabel` map. Tests: stable order across the boundary, the year appears for a previous year, and two Septembers never match.

### [P1] On a Spanish page the record kept English dates, English times and an English deck
- **What**: with the language set to `es` and `html lang="es"`, the stream read `Saturday, October 24` (eyebrow), `Today` / `Yesterday` (markers), `8:40 AM` (times) and `The small, faithful work of these days — …` (deck), while the h1, the filter button and the kind labels were Spanish. The markers' month names were Spanish only because the *browser* locale was `es-ES`; a Spanish reader on an English device got English months too.
- **Why it matters**: the page is read by Spanish-first staff, and “Hoy · 24 de octubre” next to an English sentence reads as two products stitched together. The time format is the worse half: `8:40 PM` is ambiguous to a reader who expects `20:40`.
- **Fix**: header date, day markers and clock all follow the app language (`sábado, 24 de octubre`, `Hoy`, `9:03`), and the deck and sheet copy go through `t()`. The *sentences inside the stream* — the ~20 `humanize()` verbs and `<Translate>`'d details — are still English because they are data vocabulary, not chrome; that is a different job and is filed as **#1826**.

### [P1] The phone dropped the search the desktop has
- **What**: the desktop `/history` renders a “Find a moment or a name…” input; the phone's filter sheet offered only Kind and Team member. `q` and `setQ` were live state in `History` and were never passed to the mobile branch — the phone had no way to reach them. In the sheet's control inventory there was no `INPUT` at all.
- **Why it matters**: this is the one screen whose job is *finding a past moment* (“when did we last pray for her?”). With 100-row pages and 6.4 screens for 31 moments, scrolling is the only strategy, one-handed, with the list behind a bottom bar.
- **Fix**: the search field is in the sheet, bound to the same `q`, with the existing `history.find_moment` key and a removable `“query”` chip beside the other filters. Tests: typing calls `setQ`; the chip clears it.

### [P1] Thirty-one clickable rows, none of them focusable
- **What**: every moment was a `<div onClick>` — `role=null`, `tabindex=null`, `cursor: auto` — with the person's name a `<span>` styled to look like a link. The page's tab order was: 6 shell controls, “Filter history”, 3 bottom-bar controls, then `BODY`. **0 of 31 rows** were reachable, and the whole row silently opened a person's page.
- **Why it matters**: the only interactive content on the screen was invisible to a keyboard and announced to a screen reader as static prose. The pointer affordance was missing too — no cursor change, no underline on the name until you already knew to click.
- **Fix**: the person's name is a real `<button>` (focusable, named for the person, `Enter`/`Space` work) inside the row; the generous row-wide pointer target stays, with `stopPropagation` so one tap fires once, and the row takes `cursor-pointer` only when it leads somewhere. Test: `getByRole('button', { name: 'Jane' })` resolves and opens once.

### [P1] The filter sheet was not a dialog, its close button was 28px, and “Apply filters” applied nothing
- **What**: the sheet had `role=null`, `aria-modal=null`, no `aria-labelledby`; the close control measured **28×28**; the kind chips **34px**; applied-filter chips **30px**. Choosing a kind filtered the list immediately (the sheet just covered it), so the scrim click, the X and the ✕ chips all *kept* the change while the primary button promised to “Apply”.
- **Why it matters**: on a phone this is the only route to a moment. A 28px target next to a 44px standard is a mis-tap, and “Apply filters” taught the reader that closing without applying would discard their choice — the opposite of what happened.
- **Fix**: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, `aria-labelledby` on the sheet title, Escape closes; close 44×44, kind chips and applied chips 40px tall with `aria-pressed` on the chosen kind; the primary control now says **Done** (the filters are live) and Reset all clears kind, person and query.

### [P1] An empty record was told it had filtered something out
- **What**: with **zero** activities in the app, the phone said **“No logged moments match these filters.”** — there were no filters and nothing had ever been logged. The same sentence appeared during load, because the mobile branch was never told `loading`. Neither state offered a next step, and a filter that emptied the list offered no way back.
- **Why it matters**: a new team's first visit to its own record reads as a malfunction. And a reader who narrows to “Steps forward + Caleb” gets a dead end with the only remedy being to remember the two chips are the filters.
- **Fix**: three states — gathering (`history.gathering_last_few_days`), never-logged (“No moments yet — the first thing you write down will land here.”), and filtered (“Nothing here yet for that filter” + **Show everything**). Tests: each sentence renders in its own state, and the empty-record state never says “filters”.

### [P1] The time was the least legible text on the page
- **What**: `.hist-time` measured **11px at 3.72:1** (`text-on-surface-variant/75` on the page's `#F4F4F5`), and the header eyebrow **12px at 4.16:1** (`/80`). Both fail AA (4.5:1). Every other line passed: verbs 6.12:1, quotes 7.03:1, deck 5.42:1.
- **Why it matters**: on a record, *when* is half the information. The two weakest lines were the timestamp of every moment and the page's own date.
- **Fix**: both to the full token — **7.03:1** at 11px and 12px — and the same in dark (measured 9.25:1 and 8.77:1 before the change; the token resolves per theme).

### [P1] Clearing a prayer was filed under Conversations
- **What**: `clearPrayer` writes the action `cleared a prayer for` (`src/lib/prayers.ts:380`). `humanize()` had no branch for it, so it hit the fallback: **Conversations** bucket, pencil icon, and excluded from the Prayer filter — measured on the seeded row “Tony cleared a prayer for Lila Mora.” The fallback also skips translation, so it stayed English on the Spanish page.
- **Why it matters**: the filter is the page's only grouping, and a prayer event that hides from the Prayer filter makes the record wrong about what the team did.
- **Fix**: a `prayer` branch with a heart node and “cleared a prayer for”, plus the logged burden as the quote (“Laid down …”). Test: the action files under `prayer`, and the other four buckets still resolve.

## Minor Observations

- **335px of chrome** before the first marker: a 198px header (eyebrow + 32px h1 + 3-line deck), a 44px full-width filter button, and 28px of gaps. The first moment's words begin at y=408 on an 812 viewport.
- The eyebrow (`Saturday, October 24`) and the first marker (`Today · October 24`) say the same thing 20px apart. The eyebrow is the only date if the record is empty, which is why it stayed.
- The phone shows no totals and no end-of-record cue; the desktop closes with a figures card (“31 moments noted · 4 people remembered · 4 hands at work · We write it down so nothing — and no one — is forgotten”). At the bottom of the phone list there is 350px of empty canvas and then the bottom bar.
- The header counts (`31 moments … touching 4`) come from the loaded window (`windowSize`, 100), so past 100 moments they would describe the newest page while reading as the whole record. `hasMore` treats a full window as “there is more”, so a record of exactly 100 offers a second page that may be empty.
- Row height: median 68px, longest **155px** (a six-line prayer quote). 4 of 31 rows exceeded 130px, which is the density cost of the witness quote — worth it, but it is why 31 moments is 6.4 screens.
- Date labels are set in Literata (`font-serif`) at 18px, which DESIGN's Scripture Exclusivity Rule reserves for scripture. It is the page's established voice across the app, so it is noted, not changed.
- `data-role="ft"` is hardcoded on the page container, so any other role that ever reaches this page would be styled as a Full-timer.
- The desktop branch still hardcodes “across the team, touching” in its deck sentence; the phone's is now a single translated string.
- The bottom-right “Leave a note” FAB covers the last row's timestamp when the list is scrolled to the end; global chrome, already tracked (#1785).

## Questions to Consider

- **Should a Trainee reach Looking back at all?** The epic's brief assumed yes; the role matrix says no, while `ROUTE_MIN_ROLE['/history']` says `manager`. Which is authoritative, and is the team's activity record something a Trainee should read? Filed as **#1825** rather than changed here.
- **Should the record be searchable by *content* or only by author and kind?** The desktop searches the action text and description; the phone now does the same. If the team's record grows to thousands, that needs an index, not a filter over 100 rows.
- **Does a moment need its kind announced**, not just coloured? Four node colours and four adjectives, no label. A legend, an `aria-label` on the node, or a kind word in the sentence are three different answers.
- **What should the phone do at the end of the record?** The desktop's figures card is the page's one summary. On a phone it could be the natural footer — or the list could simply say “that's everything since March”.
- **Is “6.4 screens for 31 moments” the right density?** Truncating the witness quote to two lines with an expand would halve the scroll, at the cost of the quote's weight. That is a design decision, not a bug fix.
