# Critique: Your notes — the submitter's side of the loop, at 375×812

Target: `/feedback`, signed in as `trainee.e2e@example.com` (Zion Adeyemi, `manager`) at 375×812.

**Access: the route opens for a Trainee.** `/feedback` is in the Trainee allowlist (`src/lib/permissions.ts`), the sidebar renders "Your notes", and the page loaded with the Trainee's own notes. No role question, no permission change.

The page is the last stop of the note flow: the global FAB ("Leave a note for the team") → "We got your note." → "See your notes" → here. It is also where the reply notification lands (`link: '/feedback'`).

## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** This is not a generic ticket list. The card is built around one note said in the submitter's own words, in the submitter's own language, with a coloured spine whose tone is the *kind* of note and a status sentence written for a person rather than a database ("We have this — no news yet" deliberately promises no future event, because a note with no linked issue can never reach an outcome). ADR 0019's decision that the page stops being a composer and becomes "where a Note comes back to you" is visible in the markup: there is no second composer, and the intro says where a new note comes from.

Where it drifts is not visual, it is informational. The loop's payoff — the Follow-ups — was appended to the card as a text link and left unsummarised, so the page's own purpose (what became of this?) is answered only for the outcome half, never for the conversation half. On a Spanish page, the three loudest strings (kind, outcome, and the outcome sentence) were still English, which makes the specificity read as a template when it is not.

**Deterministic scan.** `impeccable detect` is not installed in this worktree, so no CLI scan was run. `npm run check:colors` (the repo's own token gate) skipped itself — it only inspects changed files — so the hard-coded `text-green-700 dark:text-green-400` in this file was found by reading, not by the gate.

**Visual overlays.** Two live renders in English (light and dark), one in Spanish, the seeded thread expanded, and the FAB's success panel with its API stubbed. The seeded notes are fixtures written into the emulator (`feedback/e2e-note-a…d` + two `replies`): one answered by the team, one not planned, one with no outcome, one containing a 130-character URL.

## Overall Impression

It reads like a page, not a feed: one idea per card, a spine, a date, your words, then the answer. Three of the four things that were wrong on it were wrong in the same direction — the page knew more than it said. It knew the team had replied (`awaitingReply: false`) and said "no news yet". It knew a long word did not fit and hid the rest behind `overflow-x: hidden`. It knew the reader's language for the page title but not for the card's vocabulary.

All four fixes are small and local: one derived boolean, one class, one label, one set of keys. Nothing here needs a redesign, and nothing here is a reason to add a feature.

## What's Working

1. **The card's shape.** Kind chip, date, your words, the outcome — four facts in a rhythm that survives a phone. The spine says "this is a kind of note" before you read a word of it.
2. **The honesty of the outcome copy.** "We looked into this and aren't planning to build it right now, but thank you for speaking up." A no, said like a person, and the written-at-close message takes precedence when there is one.
3. **The reply composer's warning.** "Replies are posted to our public issue tracker, where anyone can read them." sits above the box, before anything is typed — the one place a submitter could be surprised by going public.
4. **Failure states that tell the truth.** A failed load is a banner, not an empty list; a failed reply keeps your text and says "That didn't send."
5. **The handoff exists.** "See your notes" in the success panel goes to this page, and the note you just left is at the top of it.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The page said "no news yet" about a note the team had answered
- **What**: the status line was `note.outcome ? outcome : "We have this — no news yet"`. A note with **two** reply records (one from the team, one from the submitter) and no outcome therefore read **"We have this — no news yet"**, and its card was pixel-identical to a note nobody had touched. Verified on the seeded `e2e-note-b` thread: two rows in `feedback/{id}/replies`, nothing on the card, the tag `Follow-ups` identical on all three.
- **Why it matters**: this is the page a reader opens to answer "did anyone get back to me?", and it is where the reply notification links. Saying "no news" while the news sits in a collapsed thread is the same class of lie as the Coordination Notes "Saved" on a lost edit. The app already knew: `addFollowUp` writes `awaitingReply: false` when the team has the last word (`src/server/feedbackPipeline.ts`), and the submitter's page never read it.
- **Fix**: derive `teamReplied = note.awaitingReply === false` (the field's only writers are submitter→`true`, team→`false`), show **"The team replied"** in the accent tone in the status slot instead of "no news yet", and label the control **"Read the reply"**. No schema change, no extra subscription: the field was already on the document the page reads. Tests: the replied state renders and "no news yet" does not; a submitter-only thread still reads "no news yet".
- **Not fixed here**: a count ("2 follow-ups") and an unread dot need reply metadata the note document does not carry — filed as **#1854**.

### [P1] A long word was silently cut off, on the page that shows you your own words
- **What**: the message paragraph was `whitespace-pre-wrap` with no break opportunity. A note containing a 130-character URL measured `scrollWidth` **734px** inside a **305px** measure, and the card's `overflow-x: hidden` clipped the rest with no ellipsis. A note about a broken link lost the end of the link.
- **Why it matters**: the page's whole contract is "here is your note". Silently truncating it is the one content failure a notes page cannot have, and a URL is the most likely thing a Trainee pastes.
- **Fix**: `break-words` on the paragraph. Re-measured: `scrollWidth` 305 = measure 305.

### [P1] The only control on the page was 16px tall and had the same name three times
- **What**: each card's only interactive element was "Follow-ups", measured **105×16** — under even WCAG 2.2's 24px minimum — with no `aria-controls`, and the accessible name was the same string on every card. Triaging with a screen reader gave three consecutive buttons called "Follow-ups"; `aria-expanded` told you it opened *something*.
- **Why it matters**: one-handed use on a bus is this page's real context, and knowing *which* note a thread belongs to is the whole question. Nine of ten existing tests clicked it by that generic name, which is exactly the ambiguity a reader faces.
- **Fix**: a full-width **301×44** row, and the accessible name now names the note: **"Follow-ups · Oct 21, 2026"** (or "Read the reply · …"). The visible label stays inside the accessible name, so Label-in-Name still holds. Cost: each card is 22px taller (232 → 254).

### [P1] On a Spanish page, the card's three loudest strings were still English
- **What**: `kindMeta().label`, `outcomeLabel()` and `outcomeCopy()` are English constants in `src/lib/feedbackKinds.ts`, and the date used `toLocaleDateString(undefined, …)` — the *browser's* locale, not the app's language. Measured on the seeded Spanish page: title **"Tus notas"**, intro and "Seguimientos" in Spanish, then **"A request" / "Not planned" / "We looked into this and aren't planning to build it right now" / "Oct 22, 2026"**.
- **Why it matters**: a Spanish-first reader gets a half-translated card, and the half that carries the answer is the English half. It also made the page look templated in the one place it is most personal.
- **Fix**: eleven keys beside the existing feedback keys (`kind_*`, `outcome_*`, `outcome_copy_*`, `team_replied`, `read_the_reply`) in `en.json`/`es.json`, and the date formatted with the active `language`. Re-measured in Spanish: **"Una petición" / "No planeado" / "Lo revisamos y por ahora no pensamos construirlo…" / "22 oct 2026"**. The note's own text and the close message stay in the language they were written in, which is correct.

### [P1] The status sentence failed AA at 12px
- **What**: `text-on-surface-variant/80` resolved to `#707074` on `#EAEAEC` — **4.09:1** at 12px, against the 4.5:1 AA floor. (Dark was 4.56:1.) It is the line that says what happened to your note.
- **Why it matters**: it is the quietest and most important sentence in the card, and 12px at 4.09:1 is exactly the combination that disappears in sunlight on a phone.
- **Fix**: the full `--text-dim` token — **6.43:1** light, 6.34:1 dark, verified in both.

### [P1] Shell: the note button still lands on the card beneath it (not fixed here)
- **What**: the global FAB sits at `311,684 48×48` and the third card's date (`Oct 20, 2026`, x 269–340) sits at y 681 — the button covers the right ~29px of the date while you read. The bottom bar likewise clips the card in progress.
- **Why it matters**: on this page the button is the only way to act, and it is parked on the content you are reading.
- **Fix**: **none in this pass** — it is the shared mobile chrome, already tracked as **#1785** (FAB tuck) and **#1795** (shell scroll). This report does not re-file it.

## Minor Observations

- Cards separate with a 1px `#F0F0F2` hairline on a `#EAEAEC` fill, where DESIGN.md's **Fill-Over-Border** rule asks for the tonal step alone. The tonal step is already there, so the border is redundant rather than harmful.
- "Shipped" is `text-green-700 dark:text-green-400` in the component while "Not planned" and "Already in the app" use `--text-dim`/`--accent`. Hard-coded palette where the rest of the card is tokens.
- "Replies are posted to our public issue tracker, where anyone can read them." is not true for a note with **no linked issue**: the pipeline only mirrors when `note.githubIssueUrl` exists (`feedbackPipeline.ts`), so an untriaged note's thread stays in-app. It errs toward privacy, which is the right direction, but it is a false sentence on every thread. Filed as **#1855**.
- A failed load is a banner with no control, though its copy says "Try again in a moment" — the only retry is a page reload.
- The FAB's success panel auto-closed after **2506ms** measured, taking "See your notes" (the only route to this page) with it. Fine on a second visit, brisk on the first.
- The empty state is a 190px panel with a ✦ and no action; its sentence points at the corner instead of offering the action. On an empty page the FAB is on screen, so the copy is accurate — this is a missed opportunity, not a defect.
- `✦` in the empty state is read aloud by a screen reader; it is decorative and unnamed.
- The date prints the year on every note, including notes from this year: four characters spent on a row that is already tight in Spanish.
- The loading skeleton renders three cards with the right rhythm, but the container carries no `aria-busy` or live region, so a screen reader gets silence until the notes appear.

## Questions to Consider

- **Should the card carry a reply count?** It cannot, honestly, without reply metadata on the note document. `awaitingReply` can say who had the last word but not how many turns there were, which is why the fix says "The team replied" rather than "2 follow-ups". That is a schema decision (**#1854**), not a copy one.
- **Should this page have a way to reach the thread from a notification?** The reply notification links to `/feedback` and not to the note, so the reader arrives at a list and has to find it. A `?note=<id>` that opens and scrolls to the thread would make the notification land where it points.
- **Is "Follow-ups" the right public word?** ADR 0019 chose it because *thread*, *message*, *question* and *ask* are taken. It is accurate internally; to a first-time submitter it may not say "this is where you and the team talk".
- **Should a note with no linked issue promise anything?** Its thread works but never mirrors. The page currently tells every reader their reply goes public. Two truths (mirrored / not mirrored) behind one sentence is the kind of thing that should be said once, correctly.
