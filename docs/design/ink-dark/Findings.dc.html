Ink · dark · what the walk turns up

Four things dark surfaces

Content survives the inversion cleanly — cards, type, signal colours and the stage hues all hold. What does not survive is the rail's quiet feedback: in dark, three of the four values Ink uses for “slightly raised” land on the rail's own colour. Each pair below is the same markup under :root and under .dark.

The light block points --surface-container-high at --panel-2; the dark block points it at --bg-elev, which is the same value as --panel. Every surface that is already a panel therefore has no raised state in dark. In the rail that is the twelve destinations, the Settings row, the Collapse button, the bell and the impersonation eye in the chrome strip, and the profile menu, which lands on the strip at the strip's own colour. The rail's internal scrollbar has the same problem from the other end — its thumb is --outline-variant (#232327) on #161618.

.dark {
- --surface-container-high: var(--bg-elev);
+ --surface-container-high: var(--panel-2); /* #202023, as light does */
}

One line, no markup, and it restores the raised step for the whole application rather than the rail alone. --surface-container-highest wants the same treatment for the tooltip.

not a dark-only bug

The badge is text-on-primary bg-primary/15. That pairing is only coherent on top of a bg-primary fill — which is exactly where it was designed and looked at. On a resting item the 15% tint blends into the rail and the text keeps the opposite theme's ink: #0A0A0B on #38383A in dark, #FFFFFF on #D2D2D3 in light. Both land near 1.6:1.

Worth saying plainly: this is not a dark-mode regression, it is a pre-existing bug that dark makes obvious — and it defeats the point of the badge, since the count matters most on the page you are not looking at. The collapsed rail is unaffected: it reduces to a bg-primary dot, which reads in both themes.

This is the system working exactly as specified — selection is inversion, and --accent-strong flipping to #FAFAFA is the documented dark behaviour. But the step is much larger here than in light. In light, a #131316 pill sits on a page whose brightest area is already white, so it reads as one dark mark among many. In dark, a 232×44 block of near-white is the highest-luminance region on the entire screen, brighter than any heading, and the eye goes to the nav rather than to the page.

Nothing here is broken and it needs no fix to ship. It is a judgement call worth making deliberately rather than inheriting: keep it, or give the dark block a slightly restrained selected fill — #E4E4E7 is already in the palette as --accent-hover, and takes roughly a fifth off the glare while staying unmistakably selected.

4

One Bento hex is still in the search field's hover ring

rest — inset 1px var(--gs-outline) #3F3F46
Search or jump to…
hover — inset 1px #525E6F, hardcoded

GlobalSearch's hover ring is a literal #525E6F — a Bento blue-grey, sitting a few degrees off Ink's neutral axis and the same in both themes. It is subtle rather than wrong, and it is one of the fourteen hardcoded hex values the spec deliberately left in place. Worth naming because the chrome strip is one of the three places the dark walk was supposed to check.

The natural token is --accent-line, which is already a theme-dependent low-alpha ink and would give the hover ring a real light and dark value instead of one shared blue.

What held up

The inversion itself is sound. --accent-on flips correctly, so primary buttons stay black-on-white; the rail takes --panel rather than staying black, which is what the original dark note asked for and what the shipped shell does; signal colours carry over legibly, with overdue in #FF7B88 and done in #51E098 at their soft 16% containers; and the stage hues at L 0.82 / C 0.14 stay the only chroma on screen without glowing. The rail's separation from the page is about as strong in dark as in light — roughly four points of L* either way — so the flush column still reads as its own object.
