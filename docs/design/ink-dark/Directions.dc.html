Giving the rail back its edge

A black slab on a white page is the strongest move a light interface has, and dark cannot borrow it — the darkest value is already the page. Each direction below finds the separation somewhere else: in elevation, in geometry, in a literal mirror, or by removing the slab. Same seven destinations, same tokens, same 232px.

The rail steps up instead of down. Page, cards and rail become three real planes — #0A0A0B, #161618, #202023 — and the rail is the lightest large surface on screen, so it reads as the thing in front.

fill--panel-2 → #202023
edge--border → #2A2A2E
hover#2A2A2E, finally visible
costone class on the aside

Tradeoff. A smaller step than light's black-on-white, and it makes the rail lighter than the cards — the reverse of light, where rail and cards share a value.

A raised fill and a gap of page colour all the way around, with the 32px radius and the shadow. Light's slab reads as an object because it is a shape on a field; this is that move, with elevation standing in for value. It is also what the Ink canvas actually drew — a floating rail that was never built.

fill--panel-2 → #202023
shape--radius-xl 32, inset 16
lift0 8px 32px rgba(0,0,0,.66)
costshell change in App.tsx

Tradeoff. Costs 32px of content width, and the rail's list already overflows a 900px viewport — insetting it takes another 32px off the height it has to scroll in.

The literal mirror: light puts black on white, so dark puts white on black. It is unambiguously the highest-contrast answer, and it is here so the case against it can be seen rather than argued.

fill#EDEDEF
ink#0A0A0B on the rail
activeinverts back to black
costa rail-only theme block

Tradeoff. A 232px column of near-white is roughly a fifth of a 1440 screen held at full brightness — the thing dark mode exists to avoid. It also needs its own inverted token set, which is the one thing Ink was shaped to not need.

Take the slab away. The rail sits at page value with one crisp rule down its edge, and the only fill in the whole column is the selected item. Contrast comes from a hard edge and from type, not from a plane — and the cards become the only lit objects, so content leads.

fillnone — --bg #0A0A0B
edge--border → #2A2A2E
hover--panel-2 #202023
costone class on the aside

Tradeoff. It answers the opposite of what was asked — the nav stops being an object. Defensible on its own terms, and it drifts furthest from the light theme, so the two stop being the same design.

The black rail is in the design, not in the build

Documented — docs/design/ink

#0A0A0B, floating, radius 32

Shipped — NavRail.tsx

bg-surface #F4F4F5, flush

The rail you are picturing is real, but it is on the canvas rather than in the application. NavRail.tsx renders bg-surface border-r border-outline-variant and always has, across both of its commits — so in light it is #F4F4F5 on white, and the black slab with its 32px radius and its shadow only ever existed in docs/design/ink.

Which is worth knowing before choosing, because it changes what these directions are. A and D are dark-only retunes that leave light exactly as it ships. B is the designed rail finally getting built, and it wants to land in both themes — black slab in light, raised slab in dark — which is the version where the two themes are the same design and dark stops feeling like the weaker one.

If the answer is “dark should feel like light does”, B in both themes is the honest fix and the only one here that touches the shell. If it is “dark just needs more separation”, A is a one-class change and can ship this week.
