What changes when it collapses

Group labelsbecome hairline dividers — the grouping survives, the words don't
Item44×44, --radius 14 — square, not a pill; a pill at this width reads as a circle
Count badgesbecome a 7px dot — “something here” without the number
Labelsmove to a tooltip on hover and focus — not hover alone, or the rail becomes keyboard-hostile
Wordmarkdrops to the mark alone
Accountavatar only; name and role move into its menu — dropped. The rail has no account block; the avatar is mounted once by the shell in the chrome strip, for the same reason search and notifications are. The block drawn in the rail above is what was proposed, not what shipped. See docs/design/DRIFT.md #8.

The expanded rail does not fit a laptop

At 834px it clears a 900px viewport by a hair and overflows a 13″ MacBook, where the browser leaves roughly 790px. The nav list has to scroll inside the rail, with the Settings link and collapse control pinned below it — not the page. Collapsed, at 750px, it fits everywhere.

Where the preference lives

In Settings — all three

Applies on desktop. Phones always use the bottom bar.
Rail Compact Top bar

The chevron at the bottom of the rail is the quick path, and it only ever moves between Rail and Compact. Getting out of the rail entirely is a Settings decision — a one-click escape from your whole navigation is a trap, not a shortcut.

The model
type NavShell =
| 'rail'            // default
| 'rail-collapsed'
| 'topbar'
storageKey = 'cisa-ui-nav'

One flat enum, not two booleans — topbar + collapsed is not a state that means anything, and a flat enum makes it unrepresentable. It also remembers: leave the rail for the top bar, come back, and you land on the width you left.

Rules

the parts that will be got wrong if they aren't written down
Below 1024px All three states fall through to MobileNav. The preference is not consulted and not changed.
1024–1279px The rail renders collapsed whatever the preference says — 232px out of 1024 is a quarter of the screen. This is a forced collapse held in component state; it must not write back to storage, or a narrow window permanently changes what someone chose.
Short viewports The nav list scrolls inside the rail. The mark, the Settings link and the collapse control stay pinned — the way out of a state must never be the thing that scrolls off.
First paint Read storage before first render, as ThemeProvider does in its useState initialiser. Read it in an effect instead and every load flashes the wrong shell and reflows the page.
Keyboard and screen readers Collapsed items keep their aria-label when the text is gone, tooltips open on focus as well as hover, and the chevron is a real button carrying aria-expanded.
Impersonation banners ImpersonateBar and OwnerViewBanner sit at the top of the content column in both rail states, and stay full-bleed under the bar in top-bar mode. A safety banner that moves depending on a display preference is worth getting right.
Search and the avatar Live in the top strip in both rail states, and back in the bar in top-bar mode. GlobalSearch and NotificationCenter get mounted by the shell, not by the nav component, so they are written once.

Worth saying plainly: this is three navigation states to keep in sync forever. Every destination added later has to be placed in a group, given an icon that survives at 20px with no label, and checked in all three. That is the cost of the choice — it buys real flexibility, and it is not free.
