THE GESTURE — REMOVE, THEN OFFER UNDO

No confirm dialog and no in-place Remove / Keep pair. The card leaves the moment the item is chosen, and a snackbar holds the way back for five seconds — the same gesture Clear this prayer got in #706, using the UndoSnackbar this view already renders.

It earns that because the action is cheap to reverse: nothing is deleted, and putting the person back is one click. A confirm step would cost every removal a decision and still not protect anything.

Mobile follows. Its × keeps its place on the card but drops the inline Remove / Keep, so both surfaces spell the action the same way.

WHAT REMOVE ACTUALLY DOES

It adds the contact's id to cisa.prayer.hidden in this browser's local storage — exactly what Choose people does when you untick a name. The contact, their prayers, their history and their card everywhere else in the app are untouched, and nothing is written to Firestore.

· A teammate's prayer page does not change. This is your page, not the team's list.
· Signing in on another laptop brings the person back.
· Clearing site data brings everyone back.

That is why the snackbar says your prayer list while the section above the cards says People we're praying for. The two disagree, and the copy here is the smallest honest patch.

Open question for the team, not settled here: should the prayer list be shared, so removing someone removes them for everyone? That is a data-model change well past this issue — worth its own decision before the wording is settled for good.
ONE MEASUREMENT THE FOURTH ITEM FORCES

The popover opens 36 px below the trigger and, at four items with a separator, runs about 161 px. On the first card at 662 px tall the trigger sits near y ≈ 404 of a 574 px scroll area, so the menu ends level with the bottom edge — and every card below the first opens the menu against the fold or past it.

So the popover should flip above the trigger when there is not room below it. RowActions is shared by the person, visit, prayer and outreach rows, so that lands everywhere at once — and it is the one piece of this change that is not a one-line addition.
