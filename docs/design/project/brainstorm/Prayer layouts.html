Prayer · layout brainstorm

Three ways to carry a prayer over time

The current screen pairs last week → this week, which assumes every prayer resolves in seven days. But a prayer is often carried for weeks before it's answered. Each direction below treats a prayer as a thread that accumulates — same warm, people-first feel, same data, three different shapes. Pick one (or mix) and I'll build it into the real screen.

A The prayer thread a journal that grows downward

One card per person. The current ask sits up top; beneath it, every week we've carried this prayer stacks on a quiet timeline — newest at the bottom, ending the day God answers. Best for seeing the whole arc of faithfulness at a glance. Watch long threads get tall.

B Carrying cards a scannable wall, weeks as beads

A grid of compact cards — the current ask is the headline. A small row of dots shows how many weeks we've carried it; the last one turns sage when it's answered. Best for taking in everyone at once during the huddle. Watch the week-history is summarized, not fully spelled out (click to expand).

C The litany a quiet column you can pray down

The most editorial: one flowing list, name in serif, the current ask in prose. The week-by-week history reads as a few faint dated lines beneath — present but never shouting. Best for the reflective, devotional feel of a prayer journal. Watch it leans text-heavy; less visual structure than A or B.
