Who is tied to a person

The issue names three ties and the code has four. Two of them already receive a follow-up; two do not. They are not equivalent: three live on the contact document and can be read by whoever is posting, and the fourth lives in each teammate’s own preferences and cannot. That asymmetry, not the count, is what decides how the reach is built.

Added her

“the assigned/created person”

Already reached
Where
contact.createdBy, falling back to contact.addedBy
Readable
At write time — on the contact document the poster is already looking at
Today
Passed as stakeholders.createdBy from ContactDetailsModal; notified in threads.ts

The strongest tie in the product — canSeeContact treats it as the first test of whether a trainee may see a person at all. Nothing changes here.

Gospel partner

“as pair of the one who created it”

Already reached
Where
contact.coCreators — stamped at creation by stampPartners
Readable
At write time — a plain array on the contact
Today
Passed as stakeholders.coCreators; notified alongside the creator

Stamped once, at creation, from the term’s pairs. A pair formed after the contact was added is not on it — the array is a snapshot, not a live join. Worth knowing before anyone reports the partner tie as broken.

Assigned to her

“the assigned/created person”

Not reached
Where
contact.owner — the caregiver, set at creation and transferable
Readable
At write time — same document, one more field
Today
Read by canSeeContact and by the feed’s On you split — and by nothing in the notify path

The clearest bug of the four. permissions.ts has said for a year that owner is one of the three ties that let you see a person; the notify path was written with two of the three. One line: add owner to ThreadStakeholders.

Keeping her

“the one who added the contact to their sheep”

Not reached
Where
userPreferences/<uid>.personalContactIds — one array per teammate
Readable
Not at write time — it is indexed by person, not by contact
Today
Read only by the owning user, on their own My Day

Not a missing line — a different shape. To answer “who keeps Elena?” at post time you would read every teammate’s preference document and scan it. Unset means “everyone I created”, so absence is not an answer either. This tie is why the reach has two paths rather than one.

Two paths, because two shapes

Three ties are on the contact and can be pushed. The fourth is on the reader and must be pulled. Trying to run all four down one path is what makes this feature look hard.

Push — at write time

The bell, to the three ties on the contact

1Someone posts the follow-up in the contact’s Follow up thread.
2addThreadMessage reads createdBy, coCreators and owner off the contact it was handed.
3One notification document per recipient, minus the poster and anyone already mentioned.
4Each recipient dismisses their own copy. Per-person state, already tracked.

This is the path that ships today, one field short. Its per-person dismissal problem is already solved: a personal notification is deleted when set aside, a broadcast one records dismissedBy, and the bell reads at most 20. Nothing accumulates.

Cost of the fix: one field on ThreadStakeholders, one line at each of the three call sites in ContactDetailsModal.

Pull — at read time

The feed, for the people keeping her

1My Day already loads your personalContactIds — it is what draws Your sheep.
2It already subscribes to every thread message via subscribeAllThreads.
3buildAttentionItems keeps a nudge on a contact in your set — the join happens on your own screen.
4No document is written for you, so there is nothing of yours to clean up.

Both halves of the join are already in memory on My Day. The pull costs one filter clause and no reads at all, and it never exposes who keeps whom.

It also fixes the same tie for the other three, who reach the feed today only by an accident of role: a trainee’s feed takes anything a Full-timer wrote, on any contact, whether or not it is theirs.

Why not just push to the keepers too

Because it is a privacy change wearing a performance costume. The rule at firestore.rules:122 lets any Trainee or Full-timer read every teammate’s preference document, so the fan-out would technically work — a client would read the whole roster’s preferences on every post, and a Trainee’s device would learn which people every teammate is privately carrying. Nothing in the product asks for that.

The pull needs none of it: your own preferences and the thread stream are both already on your screen. And the write path would still be wrong — someone adding Elena to their My Day after the question was posted would never receive it, because the fan-out already happened.

Why a follow-up ask is not a to-do

The strongest objection to this whole design, and it deserves answering in full: the product already has a thing for “someone should contact this person”, it has better machinery, and both log modals already create it.

What the to-do already has

It is genuinely better equipped

1LogVisitModal already asks “Anything to follow up?” → “Yes, put it on the list”, placeholder “e.g. Ask after her mum on Friday” — the definition, verbatim, already shipping.
2Task carries assigneeId, dueDate, status, contactId, sourceInteractionId, createdById.
3It notifies the assignee (todos.ts:184) and an assigned pending to-do is one of only three things that reach a Trainee’s My Day today.
4A real done state, on the server, visible to everyone — everything this design had to invent.

Beside it the nudge message has no owner, no date, no done state, and lands on a feed branch that has never executed. On the machinery alone, the to-do wins outright.

Why it still loses

An ask is not an assignment

1A to-do list is personal — the things I have taken on.
2Putting someone else’s name and a date on an errand creates an obligation, whoever meant it kindly.
3A follow-up ask says “this wants doing” to everyone tied and lets someone pick it up. No owner is the feature.
4So it borrows the to-do’s resolution — explicit, shared, server-side — and refuses its assignment.

The cost is honest: this rebuilds a close that Task already has. What it buys is that a Full-timer can raise something without handing a named Trainee a deadline — and that both remain available, because the to-do path is untouched. When David does want Ana to own it by Friday, he assigns a to-do.

What this asks of the data

One field on ThreadStakeholders (owner), threaded through the three ContactDetailsModal call sites — and stakeholders added to mobile’s postThreadMessage, which passes only to and therefore notifies nobody when a Trainee posts.

Two fields on an ask — closedBy, closedAt — plus the matching allowance in firestore.rules for contacts/<id>/threads, or every close is rejected while the button looks like it worked. That is the exact failure the teams field hit in #727.

One document per person at inboxState/<uid>. No new collection on the contact, no new notification type.

What it asks of the copy

AttentionFeed’s strings are hardcoded English, unlike the namespaces around it — and so are LandingTrainee’s statuses. Every line and every verb here goes through src/locales/en.json and es.json or npm run check:i18n fails.

The word is follow up only when someone contacts the person. nudge stays the internal kind name; nothing user-facing says it.
