Info, dismissible, or open — the three ways this can go

The issue poses a two-way choice and is right that both sides are bad. Read plainly, though, the dilemma comes from an assumption neither side questions: that a follow-up sent to five people is five things. If it is one thing, the tracking question stops being a question. All three are drawn honestly below, including the one recommended.

Option A

Just information

The follow-up appears in everyone’s feed and is never resolved. It ages out with the date bucket, like every other item.

The case for it

Nothing to build and nothing to store. It is where the product already is, minus the two missing ties, and the feed’s date buckets do eventually carry things away on their own.

The tradeoff

Exactly the accumulation the issue predicts. Worse with five recipients than with two: nobody can tell an unhandled request from a handled one, so the feed teaches people to stop reading it — and a feed nobody reads makes the reach fix pointless.

Option B

Each person dismisses their own

Five copies, five dismissals. Everyone clears their own; nobody’s clearing touches anyone else’s.

The case for it

It is how the bell already works, and it works: one document per recipient, set aside deletes your own, and the panel caps at 20. Per-person state that is genuinely tracked, on the server, across devices.

The tradeoff

Five people can each clear a follow-up nobody did. Clearing means “I have read this”, but the button says “I followed up” — so the one fact everybody needs, whether Elena was actually asked, is the one fact nobody can see.

Option C — drawn as Main

One open item, closed once

The ask is one piece of state on the person, not a copy in five inboxes. Anyone tied closes it, once, for all five.

One item, five views of it
TNTomas NguyenOpen 6 days · David asked
TNTomas NguyenClosed by Sam · 20m ago
Same two rows on all five screens
The case for it

It answers the question by deleting it: there is no per-person dismissal to track, so nothing accumulates and nothing has to be reconciled across devices. And it says the thing the team actually needs to know — whether the person has been contacted, and by whom.

The tradeoff

You cannot dismiss it on your own. An ask that is not yours stays on your feed until somebody does it — which is the point, and will still occasionally be an annoyance. The asker’s Never mind is the escape hatch, and the card goes quiet rather than louder as it ages.

The accumulation, counted

Twelve trainees, a term of roughly fourteen weeks, and a modest three follow-ups posted per trainee per week — about 500 follow-ups over the term, each landing on the four ties this issue asks for. That is around 2,000 deliveries, or 168 per person.

Option A carries all 168 in one person’s feed until their buckets roll over. Option B turns the same 168 into 168 deliberate dismissals — a dozen a week of clearing things by hand, none of which tells anyone anything.

Option C is the only number that does not scale with the size of the reach: 500 closes across the whole team, 42 per person, and every one of them is a follow-up that actually happened.

The half-option worth naming, and rejecting

C plus a personal “hide this from my feed” escape hatch looks like the best of both and is the worst of both: it reintroduces exactly the per-person state C was chosen to avoid, and it does it in localStorage, where it will disagree between someone’s phone and their laptop for the rest of the term. If an open follow-up genuinely does not concern you, the fix is that you should not have been on its reach in the first place — which is a question about the four ties, and belongs on the other sheet.

The narrower version that is worth keeping: the poster choosing to send an ask to one named person rather than to everyone tied. That is the existing @mention, it already notifies only that person, and it needs nothing new.

Settled since this sheet was first drawn: closing is only the explicit button. Logging an Interaction does not close an ask and neither does replying in the thread — you may have texted them about something else entirely, and closing David’s ask silently would lose it with nobody noticing. C keeps its shared close and gives up its convenience.
