An announcement is a post, not a chat turn

The stream renders every announcement through .msgb — a 62%-wide bubble, tucked to the left, next to an avatar, in a column shaped for turn-taking. It reads as one side of a conversation that never comes back. This sheet redraws it as a post, at the real width of the thread pane, and works out what it has to carry.

The swap

Both at 1:1, in a 784px pane

784px is the thread pane on a 1440 window: 1440 less the 232 rail, the 328 conversation list and six 16px gutters — the shell’s two edges and its gap, then the same three again inside .page.msgs. The bubble uses 62% of that, so a three-line announcement wraps at 486px and leaves a third of the pane empty beside it.

Five things a post has to be able to say

Emphasis is carried by the border, not by the fill — the card sits on --panel against a white pane, and a second fill would flatten it. --warn is the announcement colour already: it is what .msgs-cluster.broadcast paints the bell.

What the person who posted it can see

Today: nothing. You post to 24 people and the app tells you no more than it tells you about a text to one. “Read by 9 of 24” is the cheapest useful thing a broadcast can offer, and the data is a per-post read set — the same shape the unread dot in the conversation list already needs.

Opened from “Read by 9 of 24” in the post footer. Two lists, no percentages, no chart — the only question a poster actually has is who still needs telling, and the answer is a list of names they can act on.

This one costs something. A read set is a write per member per post, and firestore.rules would need a path a member can write to on a room they cannot otherwise write to. If that trade isn’t wanted, the footer degrades to the acknowledgement count alone — “4 said got it” — which is a member action, needs no passive tracking, and still answers the poster’s question for the posts where it matters.
